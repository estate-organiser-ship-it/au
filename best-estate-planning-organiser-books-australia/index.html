How to choose

Start by deciding whether you want to learn or to record. If you want to understand how wills, trusts and super work before you see a solicitor, a guide such as Andrew Simpson's (written for Australia) or No Loose Ends (published in the US) suits that job. If you want to write down your own accounts, contacts and wishes so your executor can find them, you want an organiser. Among the organisers, the format matters most: The Essential Estate Planner and the Bilson Law workbook are paper only, and The Estate Organiser comes as a fillable PDF, with a printed copy as an option. Some people use one of each, a guide to learn from and an organiser to fill in.

Whichever you pick, an organiser sits alongside a valid will and powers of attorney. It does not replace them.

Questions

What is the difference between an estate planning guide and an organiser?

A guide explains how estate planning works, such as wills, probate, trusts and super. An organiser is a workbook you fill in with your own details, such as accounts, insurance, where your logins are kept and funeral wishes, so the people handling your affairs can find them. Of the books on this page, Andrew Simpson's and No Loose Ends are guides, and the other three are organisers.

Which of these books are written for Australia?

The Estate Organiser (Australian Edition) and Andrew Simpson's Wills and Estate Planning: The Essential Guide for Australians say so in their own listings. The Bilson Law workbook is made by a NSW law firm. The Essential Estate Planner and No Loose Ends are published in the US, and their listings do not say they are written for Australian law.

Does an estate planning organiser replace a will?

No. An organiser is a record of information, and the makers of The Estate Organiser and the Bilson Law workbook both say theirs is not a will or a legally binding document. Estate laws vary across Australian states and territories, so speak to a qualified professional about your will and powers of attorney.

Can I fill one in on my computer?

The Estate Organiser's digital edition is a fillable PDF, according to its page. The Essential Estate Planner and the Bilson Law workbook are listed as printed only. Andrew Simpson's book and No Loose Ends come as e-books, but they are guides to read rather than forms to fill in.
