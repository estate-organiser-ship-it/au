The honest read

What they do that we do not

Their kit is built around the sequence after a death. Their words: “what to do, who to notify and in what order, across the first 48 hours, 28 days and 6 months”. They also write that a will “doesn't cover your super (that sits in a separate trust, outside the will), your joint accounts, or the actual to-do list your family faces”, and that “This kit is the plan that goes with the will.” If a running order for the weeks after a death is the thing you are missing, that is what they say they sell.

What we do that they do not

The Estate Organiser is the record itself. 337 pages with thousands of fillable fields for accounts, property, super, insurance, digital access, funeral wishes and final instructions, so the people you love are not left guessing about what exists or where it is. It comes as a fillable PDF you type into on your own computer, or a coil bound book posted to you that you fill in with a pen. Nothing is stored online. We do not hold your information and we cannot see it, which matters more here than anywhere else, because estate details are the most sensitive records a person keeps. Their home page does not say where a buyer's information is kept, so treat that as a question worth asking them rather than a point against them.

How to choose

Ask what is actually missing in your house. If nobody could list the accounts, find the policy numbers or open the phone, you need the record, and that is us. If the record already exists and what you want is the order of operations for the first weeks, look at what they describe. The two are not really the same job, and buying one does not stop you buying the other.

Questions people ask

Are The Estate Organiser and The Estate Kit the same thing?

No. They are two separate products from two separate Australian businesses. The names are similar, which is why this page exists. The Estate Organiser is published by Prestige Worldwright Pty Ltd at theestateorganiser.com.au. The Estate Kit is at au.theestatekit.com and is also marketed as the Aussie Estate Kit.

What does each one actually cost?

The Estate Organiser is $79 AUD for the digital fillable PDF, $139 for the printed coil bound book with free shipping in Australia, and $149 for printed plus digital. One time, no subscription. The Estate Kit lists three packs on its home page: “$49 AUD”, “$79” and “$129”, and says “One-off payment in AUD, no subscription” (au.theestatekit.com, 6 September 2026).

Where does my information end up?

With The Estate Organiser, on your own machine. You type into the PDF on your computer and save it there, or you print the book and write in it by hand. Nothing is stored online by us and we cannot see what you have written. The Estate Kit home page does not state where a buyer's completed information is kept, so we are not going to guess at it here.

Can I get a refund?

The Estate Organiser carries a 30-day money-back guarantee on the digital edition. Printed books are made to order, so change-of-mind refunds do not apply to them, and a book that arrives damaged, misprinted or faulty is replaced free of charge. The Estate Kit home page states a “30-day money-back guarantee” and says “We'll refund you in full” (au.theestatekit.com, 6 September 2026).

Do I need both?

Plenty of families would use both without any clash. One is the record of what you own and where it is. The other, in their own words, is “the plan that goes with the will” (au.theestatekit.com, 6 September 2026). If you want to try our side of it first, the Executor Toolkit is free.
