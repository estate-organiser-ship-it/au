The plan

A will says who gets what. It doesn't say what to do next.

The Organiser carries the whole path your executor walks, in the order they meet it. Every step below is already written out in the book, and the first stage is in the free Executor Toolkit.

The gap

What you leave sits in three systems that don't talk to each other.

No bank, no fund and no government department is in charge of joining them up. That job lands on the people you love, in the middle of their grief.

The will

It names who inherits. It does not tell anyone where the accounts are, what the passwords are, or what you wanted. Your executor can hold a valid will and still have no idea where to start.

In the Organiser: where the will is kept, who the executor is, and the message written to them.

Superannuation

Super does not automatically follow the will, and old funds from old jobs are the single most commonly lost asset in an estate. Most people have never told anyone the fund even exists.

In the Organiser: every fund, every policy and the beneficiary details, in one list.

Accounts and the home

Accounts, insurance, the mortgage, the utilities, the digital life. Each one is held by a different organisation, each with its own process, and none of them will call your family first.

In the Organiser: every account, every login, how the home is held, and who to contact for each.

Questions

Good to know.

I've already got a will. Do I still need this?

A will says who inherits. It doesn't tell anyone where your accounts are, what your passwords are, which super funds exist, or what you wanted for your funeral. Your executor can be holding a perfectly valid will and still have no idea where to start. The Organiser is the document that sits beside the will and answers all of that. The two do different jobs.

Isn't most of this free on the government websites?

Some of it is, and where a government service is the right answer the book points you at it. What no government site does is put your accounts, your passwords, your funds, your wishes and your contacts into one document your family can pick up and follow. The information is scattered across a dozen departments by design. The Organiser is the one place it all comes together, in your handwriting.

We're not wealthy. Is this really for us?

It matters more, not less. A large estate usually comes with a solicitor and an accountant already involved. A modest one is the one where a forgotten super fund, a joint account or a missing password does real damage, and where there is no professional standing by to sort it out. The Organiser is for the family that has to do it themselves.

Isn't it a bit morbid?

Most people say the opposite once they start. It stops being about dying somewhere around the second page and becomes about tidying up: the accounts you forgot, the subscription still billing, the fund from a job in the nineties. Readers describe finishing it as a relief. It is a practical afternoon, not a sad one.

Is this another subscription that will bill me every year?

No. One payment, once. No account to log into, no renewal, no annual fee. Updates to the document are yours to keep.

How long does it take to fill in?

You can do a first pass in an afternoon: the accounts, the funds, the key contacts, the things your family would need first. Most people then come back to it over the following weeks and finish it properly, one section at a time. There is no rush, and you can save and come back any time.

It looks big. Do I really have to fill in all of it?

No. It is big because it covers a whole life, and you have had a lot of life. But not every section applies to everyone. If your children are grown, the dependants section is a page you skip. No property overseas, skip foreign assets. Each chapter has a "does not apply to me" tick, and the contents page keeps track. Some sections take a while, some take a minute, some you never open. What matters is that every section you could need is in there.

Is my information safe?

Yes. It's a file you fill in on your own computer, or print and fill in by hand. Nothing is stored online or in anyone's cloud, so there's nothing to be breached. Where you keep it is entirely up to you.

Is this legal or financial advice?

No, and that's by design. The Estate Organiser is a place to record your information and wishes. It isn't a will, and it doesn't replace your solicitor or accountant. It simply makes sure that when your family needs those professionals, they can find everything fast. Rules vary across Australian states and territories, so confirm the specifics with your own advisors.

Does it work in my state?

Yes. It's about gathering your accounts, passwords, documents and wishes in one place, which matters everywhere. Where state-specific steps come up, it points you to the right professional rather than guessing for you.
