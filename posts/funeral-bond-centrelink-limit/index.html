Funeral Bonds and Centrelink: The $16,250 Allowable Limit

Published 7 January 2027 by Will Kimak.

Services Australia won't include the value of up to 2 funeral bonds in the assets test, as long as you don't also have prepaid funeral expenses and the amount invested is under the Funeral Bond Allowable Limit, which is $16,250 as at 1 July 2026. A funeral paid for in full under a contract, and a burial plot of any value, are also left out. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026)

What is a funeral bond?

Services Australia sometimes calls them funeral investments. They are managed investments that earn interest, and a funeral bond has all of these features:

  • the money is in an independently managed funeral fund
  • the interest is added to the capital
  • the fund can only release the money after your death
  • it can only release it to your estate or to the funeral director
  • it can only release it to pay for funeral costs.

(Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026)

How each funeral arrangement is treated

ArrangementIn the assets test?
Burial plotNot included, whatever its value
Funeral prepaid to a funeral director, with a contract saying it is paid in fullNot included
Funeral bond assigned to a funeral director for fully prepaid services, with a contractNot included
Up to 2 bonds, no prepaid funeral, total under $16,250Not included
A bond that isn't exemptCounted as a financial investment

(Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026)

For a prepaid funeral, the contract matters. Services Australia says it needs to set out the services you have paid for and state that there are no more costs to pay. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026)

The fine print on the limit

The Department of Social Services reviews the Funeral Bond Allowable Limit on 1 July each year, so the figure here is the one in place from 1 July 2026. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026)

  • A jointly owned bond counts as a single bond that you own, with the same limit as any other bond.
  • A couple can both invest in one bond, but the limit for that bond is the same as for any other, not double.
  • If you have money in a number of bonds, you can choose which 1 or 2 to make exempt, and Services Australia says it can help you work out which.

A bond that isn't exempt is counted as a financial investment. Your share of its current value and interest is an asset, and the deemed income from it counts as income. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026)

What to tell Centrelink

You must tell Services Australia about any funeral bonds you own, even if you know they are exempt. If you know a bond is exempt, you tell it how much you and anyone else invested, and whether you own it alone or jointly. If you aren't sure, you give it documents showing the current value, the amount invested, whether it is owned jointly and whether there is a contract with a funeral director. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026)

After that, Services Australia only needs to hear from you if:

  • you pay more money into the bond
  • you invest in another bond
  • the other joint owner of a bond dies
  • the total value of your financial investments goes up by $2,000 or more.

Interest earned on the bond doesn't need to be reported. Each year the fund should send a statement with the bond's current value. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026)

A short checklist for Mum and Dad's funeral papers

  1. Find every funeral bond, prepaid funeral contract and burial plot document.
  2. For each bond, note who owns it and how much was invested.
  3. Check whether there is also a prepaid funeral, because that changes how bonds are treated.
  4. Keep the yearly fund statements with the contract.
  5. Check that what Centrelink holds matches the papers.
  6. Tell the family where the papers are kept, and what Mum or Dad want. Our guide to talking about funeral wishes can help with that part.

The Financial Information Service is a free service that can inform and educate you about financial matters. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026)

Frequently asked questions

What is the Centrelink funeral bond limit?

Services Australia says the Funeral Bond Allowable Limit is $16,250 as at 1 July 2026. The Department of Social Services reviews it on 1 July each year.

Do funeral bonds count in the Age Pension assets test?

Services Australia won't include the value of up to 2 bonds as long as you don't also have prepaid funeral expenses and the amount invested is under the Funeral Bond Allowable Limit. A bond that isn't exempt is counted as a financial investment.

Does a prepaid funeral count as an asset for Centrelink?

Not if you have a contract that says you have paid in full. Services Australia then leaves out the amount prepaid to a funeral director and the amount in a bond assigned to a funeral director.

Does Centrelink count a burial plot?

No. Services Australia says it doesn't include a burial plot in your assessable assets, no matter what its value is.

Do I need to tell Centrelink about a funeral bond that is exempt?

Yes. Services Australia says you must tell it about any funeral bonds you own, even if you know they are exempt from the assets test.
