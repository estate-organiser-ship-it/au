DVA Bereavement Payment and Funeral Benefit: What Families of Veterans Can Get

Published 31 December 2026 by Will Kimak.

When a veteran who was getting a Department of Veterans' Affairs (DVA) payment dies, DVA may pay a one-off, non-taxable bereavement payment. It goes to the surviving partner if the veteran was part of a couple, and in some cases to the estate if the veteran was single. DVA may also pay a funeral benefit of up to $3,000 towards the funeral. (Source: dva.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

This guide covers veterans who were already receiving a pension or compensation under the Veterans' Entitlements Act 1986 (VEA). DVA has a separate page for payments under the Military Rehabilitation and Compensation Act 2004 (MRCA). (Source: dva.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026) Check which Act applied to your family member before you rely on anything below.

The two types of bereavement payment

DVA pays two types under the VEA: one for a veteran who received a Disability Compensation Payment, and one for a veteran who received an Income Support Payment. If the person received both, a bereavement payment may be made for each. (Source: dva.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

SituationWhat DVA describes
Couple, veteran received a Disability Compensation PaymentA payment to the partner equal to 6 fortnightly instalments at the veteran's rate
Couple, veteran received an Income Support PensionThe partner keeps the veteran's pension entitlements for 98 days, usually paid as a lump sum of the difference between the new single rate and the previous combined rates
Single veteran on an Income Support PensionPension entitlements paid to the estate for 14 days
Single veteran on Special rate (TPI) or EDA, died in needy circumstancesA payment to the estate equal to 6 fortnightly instalments, on a claim

(Source: dva.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

If the veteran had a partner

DVA pays the surviving partner in two circumstances: the veteran was receiving a Disability Compensation Payment, or the veteran was receiving a Service Pension, Social Security Age Pension or Income Support Supplement. In the second case, the partner must also be receiving a Service Pension, Income Support Supplement or Social Security Age Pension at the time of the death. (Source: dva.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

You generally do not have to apply. DVA says payment for a member of a couple occurs on notification of the death, and the lump sum is paid into the account where the surviving partner's pension is paid. If the new single rate is the same as or higher than the old combined rates, there is no extra payment. (Source: dva.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

A couple living apart because one or both were ill, frail or in respite care still counts as a couple. For a couple separated by illness, DVA works the payment out on the partnered rate as if they were not living apart. (Source: dva.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

If the veteran was single

DVA usually makes a bereavement payment to the estate if the veteran was single, separated or widowed at the time of death and was receiving one of the payments it lists. For a single veteran on Special rate (TPI) or Extreme Disablement Adjustment (EDA) Disability Compensation Payment, the veteran must also have died in needy circumstances, which DVA defines as the estate not having enough funds to pay all outstanding liabilities, including the funeral. (Source: dva.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

That payment needs a claim. The form is D9145, Claim for Bereavement Payment for Single Veterans, and it must be made within 12 months of the veteran's death. (Source: dva.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

The funeral benefit

DVA describes the funeral benefit as a one-off payment of up to a maximum of $3,000 towards the funeral costs of an eligible veteran or dependant. It can cover memorial ceremonies, burial or cremation, and transporting the veteran's body from the place of death. DVA can pay it straight to the funeral director if the executor or nominated representative asks. (Source: dva.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

Some veterans qualify without a claim: those receiving Special rate (TPI), EDA, or Disability Compensation Payment plus an allowance as a multiple amputee at the time of death, and former prisoners of war. Others may qualify if they died from an accepted service-related disability, in an institution such as a hospital or nursing home, or while being treated for a terminal illness. In those cases the estate or a dependant applies, within 12 months of the death. (Source: dva.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

From 1 July 2026 there are two rates for funeral expenses, a lower rate and a higher rate. Where a veteran qualifies without a claim, the lower rate is paid, and the family can apply under the MRCA for further funeral expenses up to the higher rate. (Source: dva.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

What to do, in order

  1. Tell DVA about the death. For a couple, the bereavement payment follows from that notification.
  2. Find out which Act applied. VEA and MRCA payments are on separate DVA pages.
  3. Single veteran in needy circumstances? Complete form D9145 within 12 months.
  4. Check the funeral benefit. Ask whether it applies without a claim, or whether the estate needs to apply within 12 months.
  5. Send forms by email to bereavement.sa@dva.gov.au or by post to GPO Box 9998, Brisbane QLD 4001, or call 1800 VETERAN (1800 838 372).

(Source: dva.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

Other support for partners and children

Partners of deceased veterans may be eligible for a Wholly Dependent Partner Pension, and dependent children for an Eligible Young Person pension, both handled under the MRCA. War widows and widowers may be entitled to Income Support Supplement, which is based on income and assets. (Source: dva.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026) Open Arms, Veterans and Families Counselling, is a free and confidential 24-hour service on 1800 011 046. (Source: dva.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

General information only. This is not financial or legal advice. DVA entitlements depend on the veteran's payments and service history, so confirm your situation with DVA directly.

Frequently asked questions

Is the DVA bereavement payment taxable?

No. DVA describes it as a one-off, non-taxable payment, and says bereavement payments are not counted as income for taxation purposes. (Source: dva.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

Do I need to apply for the DVA bereavement payment?

Generally not if the veteran was a member of a couple. DVA says payment occurs on notification of the death. A claim on form D9145 is needed for a single veteran who died in needy circumstances, within 12 months of the death. (Source: dva.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

How much is the DVA funeral benefit?

DVA describes it as a one-off payment of up to a maximum of $3,000 towards the funeral costs of an eligible veteran or dependant. From 1 July 2026 there is a lower rate and a higher rate. (Source: dva.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

How long does the surviving partner keep getting the veteran's pension?

Where the veteran received an Income Support Pension, DVA says the surviving partner continues to receive the veteran's pension entitlements for 98 days from the day the veteran died, in most cases as a lump sum. (Source: dva.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)
