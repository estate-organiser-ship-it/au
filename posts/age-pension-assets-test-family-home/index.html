Does the Family Home Count in the Age Pension Assets Test?

Published 24 December 2026 by Will Kimak.

Generally, no. Services Australia doesn't include your principal home in the Age Pension assets test, and it defines that home as the one you live in plus the first 2 hectares of land it is on, on a single title. Land over 2 hectares, a second property, and some changes such as moving into care can bring property into the test. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026)

What counts as the principal home?

Services Australia's words are short: your principal home is the home you live in and the first 2 hectares of land it is on, and it must be on a single title. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026)

Land beyond that is normally counted. Its own example is a home on 6 hectares, where 4 hectares are included in the assets test. There are exceptions for rural customers and primary producers, and any part of the home used only for business, such as a room, land or a shed, is included. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026)

The current asset limits

Services Australia sets separate limits for homeowners and non-homeowners. For a couple, the limit is for both partners' assets combined. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026)

Your situationFull pension limitPart pension cut off
Single homeowner$333,000$745,750
Single non-homeowner$600,000$1,012,750
Couple homeowner, combined$499,000$1,121,000
Couple non-homeowner, combined$766,000$1,388,000

When assets are above the full pension limit, the pension reduces, and from 20 September 2026 part pensions cancel above the cut off point. The cut off is higher for people who get Rent Assistance. The Department of Social Services reviews the limits in March, July and September each year. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026)

Property that does count

Services Australia includes most real estate you own. It lists real estate you rent out, leave vacant for any amount of time, let someone else live in for free, or live in when you are not at your principal home. That covers the holiday house and the empty unit. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026)

It values property at current market value, which is different from council rates and valuation notices. Residential property is adjusted each year using indexed market data by postcode, and other property, including a home block on more than 2 hectares, is revalued every 2 years. If you disagree with a valuation, you can ask it to reconsider. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026)

When the home can start to count

Selling it. For homes sold from 1 January 2023, the part of the sale proceeds you plan to use on a new principal home can be exempt for up to 24 months, with a possible further 12 months, to a maximum of 36 months. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026)

Moving into care. If you leave your principal home to go into aged care, Services Australia may include the home in your assets test. If you leave due to illness and enter a care situation, it may exempt the home for 2 years from the date you enter care, after which the home is assessed as an asset and you are assessed as a non-homeowner. It won't count the home if your partner is still living there, or if you leave temporarily. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026)

Giving it away. Services Australia says that if you give away your income or assets, they may still count towards your income and assets tests, and that this also applies if you sell them for less than they are worth. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026) Our guide to Centrelink gifting rules covers this.

A life interest. If a will gives someone the right to keep living in a house, Services Australia doesn't include that life interest in their assets test, because it treats the place as their principal home. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026)

A quick check for your family

  1. List every property owned, in full or in part.
  2. Mark which one is the principal home, and check it sits on a single title.
  3. Find the land area. Anything over 2 hectares is normally counted.
  4. Note any part of the home used only for business.
  5. Compare the other assets with the limits on the Services Australia page on the day you check.
  6. Before selling, moving into care or transferring the home, talk to Services Australia.

The Financial Information Service is a free service that can inform and educate you about financial matters, and it is a good place to start. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026)

General information only, not legal advice. How a home is assessed depends on the whole of a person's situation, including farm land, care arrangements and who else lives there. Please confirm with Services Australia, and get financial or legal advice before selling or transferring property.

Frequently asked questions

Does the family home count in the Age Pension assets test?

Generally no. Services Australia says the only real estate asset it doesn't include is your principal home, which is the home you live in and the first 2 hectares of land it is on, on a single title.

What is the 2 hectare rule for the Age Pension?

Services Australia normally includes land over the first 2 hectares your home is on. In its example, a home on 6 hectares has 4 hectares included in the assets test. There are exceptions for rural customers and primary producers.

What are the Age Pension asset limits for homeowners?

On the Services Australia page, the full pension limit is $333,000 for a single homeowner and $499,000 combined for a homeowner couple. From 20 September 2026 the part pension cut off is $745,750 for a single homeowner and $1,121,000 combined for a couple.

Does the home count if Mum moves into aged care?

Services Australia says it may include the home if a person leaves it to go into aged care. If they leave due to illness and enter a care situation, it may exempt the home for 2 years. It won't count the home while a partner still lives there.

Does Centrelink use the council valuation of the house?

No. Services Australia uses current market value, which it says is different from council rates and valuation notices. Residential property is adjusted each year using indexed market data by postcode.
