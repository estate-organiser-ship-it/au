The Date of Death Tax Return: What the ATO Asks of an Executor

Published 10 December 2026 by Will Kimak.

A date of death tax return is the final individual tax return for a person who has died. It covers the period from 1 July of the income year in which they died up to the date of death, and the ATO says it is usually lodged by the authorised legal personal representative (LPR), which is generally the executor or administrator. (Source: ato.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026)

When is a date of death return needed?

The ATO says you must lodge one if any of these applied to the person in the income year they died:

  • they had tax withheld from their income, including from interest or dividends
  • their taxable income was above the tax-free threshold
  • they lodged tax returns in the years before their death, or had outstanding tax returns.

If there are outstanding returns for earlier years, those need lodging too, and the ATO can tell you about them when you notify it of the death. If no return is needed, you let the ATO know with a non-lodgment advice form, printing DECEASED and the date of death where it asks for the reason. (Source: ato.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026)

Who can lodge it?

You can lodge it once you have notified the ATO of the death and been entered on its records as the person managing the deceased's tax affairs. (Source: ato.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026) To be treated as the authorised LPR, the ATO says you need a grant of probate or letters of administration issued by a court. (Source: ato.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026) Without one, the ATO can still help, but there are legal restrictions on the information and funds it can release to you. (Source: ato.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026)

Where there is no will and no one has been appointed to administer the estate within 6 months of the death, the ATO says it may raise an assessment of the estate's tax-related liabilities and seek to recover any tax owing. (Source: ato.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026)

The steps, in order

  1. Pause the letters. Phone the ATO to say the person has died and it will stop sending tax correspondence to them. (Source: ato.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026)
  2. Notify the ATO officially. One form covers the death and who will manage the estate. You can do it online and then attend an interview at a participating Australia Post outlet, or post a paper form with certified copies of your documents. You need the death certificate, or a grant of probate or letters of administration. If you plan to apply for probate, the ATO suggests waiting for the grant before you submit, to avoid delays. (Source: ato.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026)
  3. Gather the tax information. If the papers at home don't have what you need, an authorised LPR can get the person's tax information and tax file number from the ATO. (Source: ato.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026)
  4. Prepare the return on paper. The ATO says you lodge it on the paper tax return for individuals, not through myTax or myGov, because those can only be used by the account holder for their own return. If the authorised LPR has appointed a tax agent, the agent can prepare and lodge it online. (Source: ato.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026)
  5. Mark it as a deceased estate. Write DECEASED ESTATE at the top of the first page, give the name as LEGAL REPRESENTATIVE OF the person's name (DECEASED), answer No to whether they will need to lodge in future, and sign on their behalf. (Source: ato.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026)
  6. Provide for any tax owing before anything is distributed to beneficiaries.

Date of death return or trust tax return?

These are two different returns, and families mix them up.

Date of death returnTrust tax return for the estate
Whose incomeThe person who diedThe deceased estate
Period1 July of that income year to the date of deathAfter the death, until the estate is finalised

The ATO treats income the estate receives after the death separately from the person's own income. An estate may need trust tax returns if it earns income such as rent or dividends. (Source: ato.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026) (Source: ato.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026)

What goes into it

  • Income earned and deductible expenses incurred up to the day the person died.
  • The cost of preparing the return, such as tax agent fees paid by the executor or administrator, even when they are incurred after the death.
  • Capital gains and losses from CGT events that happened before the death.

Tax and capital losses can't be carried forward to the estate, and lapse if they can't be used in this return. A study loan such as HELP doesn't need to be included, as it is assessed separately, and any debt left after a final repayment is cancelled. (Source: ato.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026)

Refunds and tax owing

The notice of assessment comes to you. The ATO says you must provide for any tax owing before distributing the estate's assets, and if this isn't done you may be personally liable. Any refund or franking credits are released to the authorised LPR. (Source: ato.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026) On a brighter note, the ATO's own checklist says there are no inheritance taxes in Australia. (Source: ato.gov.au, checked 16 Nov 2026)

The ATO's date of death page doesn't set out a due date of its own, so ask the ATO or a registered tax agent when yours is due.

General information only, not legal advice. This is not tax advice either. Every estate's tax position is different. If the person ran a business, held investments or died with returns outstanding, please speak to the ATO or a registered tax agent before you lodge.

Frequently asked questions

What is a date of death tax return?

It is the final tax return for a person who has died. The ATO says it covers the period from 1 July of the income year in which the person died up to the date of death.

Does every estate need a date of death return?

No. The ATO says one must be lodged if the person had tax withheld from their income, had taxable income above the tax-free threshold, or lodged returns in earlier years or had returns outstanding. If none applies, you send a non-lodgment advice instead.

Who lodges the date of death return?

Usually the authorised legal personal representative, generally the executor or administrator. You can lodge once you have notified the ATO of the death and been entered on its records as the person managing the deceased's tax affairs.

Can I lodge a date of death return through myTax?

No. The ATO says it is lodged on the paper tax return for individuals because myTax and myGov can only be used by the account holder. A tax agent appointed by the authorised legal personal representative can lodge it online.

Is there inheritance tax in Australia?

The ATO says there are no inheritance taxes in Australia. Tax may still be owed on the person's income up to the date of death, and the estate may need its own trust tax returns.
