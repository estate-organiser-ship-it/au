How to Find a Deceased Person's Lost Super in Australia

Published 17 December 2026 by Will Kimak.

To find a deceased person's super, start with any fund you know about, then check whether the ATO is holding super for them. Super funds must pay unclaimed super to the ATO for members who have died where the fund has been unable to pay the benefit to the rightful owner, and the ATO says a beneficiary of a deceased person may be able to claim it. (Source: ato.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

What the ATO can tell you depends on whether you hold a grant of probate or letters of administration. With one, you can see the person's full tax and super information. Without one, the ATO's own guidance is that what it can share is limited. (Source: ato.gov.au and ato.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

Why super goes missing

People change jobs, move house and stop opening fund letters. The ATO says that when a person dies, their family members and other beneficiaries might not know if they had any super or where that super may be held. (Source: ato.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

Super is also handled outside the will. The ATO describes it as generally not part of a deceased person's estate, managed by super funds and paid according to the fund's rules, with any valid beneficiary nomination playing a key role. In some cases, part or all of the balance may have been transferred to the ATO. (Source: ato.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

Step by step: finding it

  1. Contact every fund you know of. The ATO says to notify the person's super fund with a certified copy of the death certificate and any other documents the fund needs, and to ask it to release the super. (Source: ato.gov.au and ato.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)
  2. Tell the ATO you are managing the estate. The ATO says this is the first step if you are trying to finalise the person's tax. (Source: ato.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)
  3. If you hold probate or letters of administration, ask for the data package. The authorised legal personal representative can be sent a deceased estate data package. It includes details of any superannuation accounts, plus three years of tax return information, notices of assessment and any debts with the ATO. (Source: ato.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)
  4. Check for ATO-held super. This includes unclaimed super money paid in by funds and money in the Superannuation Holding Account. (Source: ato.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)
  5. Claim any ATO-held amount on the paper form, with the deceased person cover sheet, as set out below.

What changes without probate

This is the part that surprises families. The ATO says an executor who has not obtained a grant of probate is not an authorised legal personal representative, and there are legal restrictions on what it can disclose to someone who is not. That includes a family member without letters of administration and an executor without a grant of probate. (Source: ato.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

For super specifically, when a relative or executor without a grant asks about a deceased person's super, the ATO sends a Lost Super Enquiry email to the person's fund with the contact details of whoever is asking. The ATO's notes to funds say the legislation prevents it from telling the applicant whether any relevant super accounts exist, and the applicant will not know whether an email was sent. After that, the ATO has no visibility of what the fund does. (Source: ato.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

In practice, without a grant you may be waiting on a fund to contact you. Whether to apply for probate is a question for a solicitor or your state's probate registry, because the rules differ between states and territories.

Claiming super the ATO is holding

The ATO says the Commissioner is required by law to pay unclaimed super owed to a deceased person to a beneficiary named under a valid binding death nomination, and if there is no valid nomination, to the person's legal personal representative. (Source: ato.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

The claim is made on the paper Application for payment of ATO-held superannuation money (NAT 74880). For a deceased person, you must include the deceased person cover sheet (NAT 75530) with the main application. Copies of documents go to the Australian Taxation Office, PO Box 3578, Albury NSW 2640, and the ATO asks you not to send originals or use pins or staples. (Source: ato.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

What you send depends on who you are:

If you areThe ATO asks for
Executor or administrator with an Australian grantThe death certificate, plus probate, letters of administration or a notice of public trustee
Executor without an Australian grantThe death certificate, a copy of the will, and documents explaining why probate or letters of administration is not being sought
Family memberThe death certificate, and documents proving you have authority to act for the estate or have taken on that role
Potential beneficiaryThe death certificate, documents identifying other potential beneficiaries and their relationship to the person, and a binding death nomination letter or a letter from the fund confirming your entitlement

(Source: ato.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026) If the ATO already has formal notification of the death from Births, Deaths and Marriages, a copy of the death certificate is not required. The ATO also notes that it may not be able to release super without probate or letters of administration, and that a claim in a deceased estate can be delayed while it asks super funds about the person's nominated beneficiaries. (Source: ato.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

Certified copies

Copies of most documents must be certified by an authorised person, who sights the original and writes "certified true copy" with their qualification, such as Justice of the Peace. The ATO will not accept copies certified by someone with an ongoing relationship with the claimant, such as a family member, business partner or the claimant's tax agent. (Source: ato.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

Frequently asked questions

How do I find out if a deceased person had super?

Contact any fund you know about with a certified death certificate. The authorised legal personal representative can also get a deceased estate data package from the ATO, which includes details of any superannuation accounts. (Source: ato.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

Can the ATO tell me about a deceased person's super without probate?

Only in a limited way. Without a grant, the ATO sends a Lost Super Enquiry email to the person's fund with your contact details. It says the legislation prevents it from telling you whether any relevant super accounts exist. (Source: ato.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

Who gets unclaimed super held by the ATO when someone dies?

The ATO says the Commissioner is required by law to pay it to a beneficiary named under a valid binding death nomination, and if there is no valid nomination, to the person's legal personal representative. (Source: ato.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

What form do I use to claim ATO-held super for a deceased person?

The Application for payment of ATO-held superannuation money (NAT 74880), with the deceased person cover sheet (NAT 75530) included, sent with copies of your supporting documents. (Source: ato.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

Is super part of the estate?

The ATO says super is generally not considered part of a deceased person's estate and must be handled separately. It is paid according to the fund's rules, with any valid beneficiary nomination playing a key role. (Source: ato.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)
