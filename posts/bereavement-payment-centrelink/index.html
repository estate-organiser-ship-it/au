Centrelink Bereavement Payment: What You Can Get When Your Partner Dies

Published 3 December 2026 by Will Kimak.

If your partner died and you were both getting a Centrelink pension, or an income support payment for at least the previous 12 months, Services Australia may pay you a lump sum bereavement payment to help you move to a single income. The amount is usually what the two of you would have got as a couple, minus your new single rate, worked out over 14 weeks from the day your partner died. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

There is no separate claim form for this one. Services Australia says that when you tell it about the death, it works out whether you are eligible and how much you can get. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026) Below is who qualifies, the other bereavement payments that sit beside it, and what to have ready when you call.

Who can get it

Services Australia says the type and amount of any payment when someone dies depends on your circumstances, your relationship to the person who died, and when you tell it about the death. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026) For a surviving partner, the main rule is that you both needed to be getting an income support payment for at least the previous 12 months, or a pension. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

A few situations have their own rules:

  • JobSeeker Payment or Youth Allowance. You may get a lump sum if you are getting or claiming one of these when your partner dies, and you were both Australian residents at the time of the death. If you are pregnant when your partner dies, an extra amount is paid, and you need to give medical evidence including the expected date of birth.
  • ABSTUDY Living Allowance or Farm Household Allowance. If you have been getting either for 12 months or more, you may get a lump sum bereavement payment if your partner dies.
  • Not on any Centrelink payment. You may be eligible for an income support payment such as JobSeeker Payment or Youth Allowance, which you can claim online or by phone.

(Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

How the amount is worked out

There is no fixed dollar figure on the Services Australia page, because the payment depends on what the couple was receiving. Its description is this: the amount paid is usually equal to the total you and your partner would have got as a couple, minus your new single rate, calculated over a 14 week period that starts on the day your partner died. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

So the gap between the couple rate and the single rate is what the payment covers, for a set window. Once the 14 weeks are over, you are on your own single rate. If the numbers matter for a decision about the house or savings, Centrelink's free Financial Information Service is one place to talk it through.

What to do, in order

  1. Tell Services Australia about the death. Its Death of a loved one page says you need to tell it within 28 days if someone has died. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)
  2. Use the Advice of death form if you prefer paper. Form SA116 is used to advise of the death of an adult or child, and the details are used to update Medicare, Centrelink and Child Support records. The form page asks for it to be returned within 28 days of the death, printed and signed by hand. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)
  3. Or phone. The Centrelink older Australians line, 132 300, Monday to Friday 8 am to 5 pm, lists payments, information and help following the death of a loved one among the things it handles. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)
  4. Let Services Australia work out the payment. When you tell it about the death, it works out whether you are eligible and how much you can get. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)
  5. Ask about cards. A new Medicare card or concession card is not issued unless you ask, and a new Medicare card will not show the name of the person who died. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

If your partner was in the Pension Bonus Scheme

This is a separate payment with its own form. Services Australia says you may be eligible for a Pension Bonus Bereavement Payment if your partner registered for the Pension Bonus Scheme, with Services Australia or the Department of Veterans' Affairs, and died before making a successful claim for Age Pension and Pension Bonus. In this case you do not need to be getting an income support payment, and the payment is based on the amount of Pension Bonus your partner would have got. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

The conditions on the partner are specific. They must have been a Pension Bonus Scheme member for at least 12 months, not claimed Age Pension and Pension Bonus before they died, not received an income support payment other than Carer Payment or Carer Service Pension since first qualifying for Age Pension, and passed the work test rules for at least 12 months from when their membership started. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

The deadline is the part to write down. You submit your claim within 26 weeks of your partner's death, unless you get an extension. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026) The claim is a paper form you download, ask the older Australians line to post, or request at a service centre. You will need confirmation of your partner's birth date and date of death, and proof they passed the work test. It goes back by mail or in person at a service centre, and you get a letter with the outcome. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

If you were the carer

If you get Carer Payment for a person who dies, you may keep getting it for up to 14 weeks after they die, and you may also get a lump sum bereavement payment depending on your circumstances. If you get Carer Allowance for an adult who dies, you may get a lump sum of up to 14 weeks of Carer Allowance, as long as you get an income support payment that does not include an extra payment when someone dies and you do not get Carer Payment. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026) Our guide to Carer Payment after a death goes through that in more detail.

If the person who died was single

Services Australia says there are no extra lump sum payments when a single person dies. A single pensioner is paid the regular payment covering the fortnight in which they died, and the executor can access it from their bank account. For a single person on an allowance such as JobSeeker Payment, the final payment runs up to the day before their death. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

If you are the executor and think Services Australia owes money to the person who died, you can ask it to check. That needs the Executor/Administrator Request for information form and proof that you are the executor or administrator, and each person listed as an executor or administrator must complete the form. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

General information only. This is not financial advice. Centrelink rules depend on your own payments and circumstances, so check your situation with Services Australia before you rely on any figure.

Frequently asked questions

How much is the Centrelink bereavement payment?

Services Australia does not publish one figure for it. The amount paid is usually the total you and your partner would have got as a couple, minus your new single rate, calculated over a 14 week period starting on the day your partner died. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

Do I need to apply for the bereavement payment?

For the partner bereavement payment, Services Australia says that when you tell it about the death, it works out if you are eligible and how much you can get. The Pension Bonus Bereavement Payment is different and needs a claim form. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au and servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

How long do I have to tell Centrelink about a death?

Services Australia says you need to tell it within 28 days if someone has died, and asks for the Advice of death form (SA116) to be returned within 28 days of the death. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au and servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

What is the deadline for the Pension Bonus Bereavement Payment?

Services Australia says to submit your claim within 26 weeks of your partner's death, unless you get an extension. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)

Is there a bereavement payment when a single person dies?

No extra lump sum is paid. A single pensioner is paid the regular payment covering the fortnight in which they died, and the executor can access it from their bank account. (Source: servicesaustralia.gov.au, checked 25 Nov 2026)
